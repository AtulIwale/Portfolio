Method, per-category validation & limitations

Join fact_booking.unit_id to dim_unit.unit_id for project and unit details, and fact_booking.customer_id to dim_customer.customer_id for lead source. Apply each raw-field rule independently. Only afterward join answer_key by booking_id to evaluate agreement.

The workbook provides one is_at_risk label. For category-specific evaluation, Y labels with “Cancellation cluster linked to …” reasons define cancellation targets; Y labels with “Low booking amount on a long-stalled enquiry in a repeat-risk project cohort.” define enquiry targets. Every category is evaluated against all 500 bookings; the other category's Y rows are negatives for that particular target. This is not a blended evaluation.

Both categories contain 10 PRJ001, 10 PRJ011 and 5 PRJ021 bookings. They are disjoint. The enquiry rule uses no amount or age threshold even though the supplied reason mentions low amounts and long-stalled enquiries. Recorded cancellations are observed statuses, not predictions of future cancellation.

Booking amount is a booking/token amount in INR, not sale value, outstanding receivables or confirmed loss. Unit list price is shown separately in evidence. This synthetic snapshot does not establish real-world customer risk. No current-clock aging or model training is used.

The original workbook is unchanged. Editing it does not automatically update embedded JSON. Integrity checks block rendering for broken joins, duplicate IDs, invalid fields or labels, unrecognized Y reasons, or a changed snapshot that no longer supports the documented category agreement.
