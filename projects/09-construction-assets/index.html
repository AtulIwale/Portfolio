Expected-life review compares the reporting date with purchase date plus expected_life_years using calendar anniversaries. February 29 anniversaries fall on February 28 in non-leap years. “Past” means strictly after that anniversary. Zero matches does not establish asset health. Method, validation & data limitations

Deterministic, not a trained model. Classification reads raw maintenance records only; the answer key is joined afterward for comparison. Sort all logs by asset and service date, inspect every consecutive three-log window, and flag the union of qualifying log IDs. Matching is case-insensitive, with all three symptom phrases required on every log; whitespace or a hyphen may separate “hydraulic” and “leakage.” This literal matcher does not understand negation or synonyms.

Only confirmed-pattern membership has a supplied ground-truth target. No precision/recall is assigned to data-quality or proactive checks, and no combined risk score is calculated. Fixed and Movable filters use the same rule.

Duplicate IDs, missing joins, invalid ratings/quantities/dates or tied service dates block rendering. Predate-purchase dates and decreasing meters remain visible review findings rather than being silently fixed or excluded from pattern detection.

The workbook is an unchanged synthetic demonstration snapshot. The latest service date fixes this report's date; no current-clock aging is used. Editing the workbook does not refresh embedded JSON automatically.
