Why ERP adoption fails on site — and what to do differently

← All insights

Start with the transaction, not the screen

A configured workflow can still fail operationally. A site engineer may know which screen to open but not which source document is required, who owns an exception, or what happens when the approver is unavailable. Adoption depends on those decisions as much as on software training.

Follow one record from start to finish

Choose a recurring transaction such as a material requisition. Trace its source, approval, purchase order, receipt and invoice. At each handoff, establish who creates the record, what must be correct, and what evidence allows the next person to act. This makes gaps in ownership and data visible before they become support tickets.

Train the exception path

Training should cover incomplete information, rejected approvals, partial receipts and corrected entries. A user who can complete only the ideal transaction remains dependent on informal support. Use role-specific tasks and let users explain the next handoff in their own words.

Define readiness with evidence

Go-live readiness requires reconciled opening data, tested role access, accepted business scenarios and an owned support process. A completed training session is one input; it is not proof that users can execute the process reliably.

Review behavior after launch

Track recurring errors and unresolved handoffs alongside technical incidents. Investigate whether a problem comes from the process, source data, interface or training. Adjust the control or guidance and verify the same transaction again. The aim is a usable operating process, not simply a completed software rollout.
