Decision intelligence

Turning procurement data into project intelligence

← All insights

Begin with a decision

Procurement reporting becomes useful when it helps someone decide what to investigate or approve. An order-value total alone cannot explain whether a package threatens the schedule, whether a receipt is incomplete, or whether an invoice is ready for payment.

Keep the grains separate

Requisitions, purchase-order lines, receipts and invoice lines represent different events. Joining them without checking cardinality can multiply quantities and values. Define the grain of each fact table, retain stable references and reconcile totals before creating summary measures.

Distinguish dates and commitments

An order date, promised delivery date and actual receipt date answer different questions. Award data can support supplier concentration analysis, but it cannot establish delivery reliability without promised and actual receipt evidence. Likewise, an approved commitment is not the same as a posted cost.

Show exceptions with their records

A useful dashboard links a variance or delay to its package, owner and source transaction. Show missing dates and incomplete references explicitly. A blank field is not a zero, and an unreceived order is not automatically a late order unless its contractual delivery point is known.

Connect the output to action

Give the procurement team a review queue with a defined next step: confirm the delivery date, reconcile a receipt, clarify an exclusion or update a forecast. Preserve the underlying evidence so that the decision can be reviewed later.
